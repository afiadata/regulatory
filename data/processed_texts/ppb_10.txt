DEFINITION OF TERMS 
Health products: include human medicines, Health Products and 
Technologies, medicinal substances, vaccines, diagnostics, medical devices, 
blood products, traditional and alternative medicine, therapeutic feeds and 
nutritional formulations, cosmetics and related products. 
Health technologies: mean the application of organized knowledge and skills 
in the form of devices, medicines, vaccines, procedures and systems 
developed to solve a health problem and improve the quality of life. 
Essential medicine: means medicine that satisfies the priority health care 
needs of the population and is selected with due regard to disease prevalence 
and public health relevance, evidence of clinical efficacy and safety, and 
comparative costs and cost-effectiveness. 
Health establishment: means the whole or part of a public or private 
institution, facility, building, or place, whether for profit or not, that is 
operated or designed to provide inpatient or outpatient treatment, diagnostic 
or therapeutic interventions, nursing, rehabilitative, palliative, convalescent, 
preventative or other health services. 
In vitro diagnostic (IVD): means an IVD as defined in the Health Act 2017. 
Medicine: means medicine as defined in the Health Act 2017. 
Medical Device: means a medical device as defined in the Health Act 2017.  
Recognized NRA: means a reference regulatory authority with which the 
pharmacy and poisons board shares a recognition agreement.

1.0 
INTRODUCTION 
1.1 
Background 
The Donation Guideline for applicants, established by the Pharmacy 
and Poisons Board (PPB), provides a structured framework for 
receiving, evaluating, and approving donations of Health Products 
and Technologies. Recognizing the importance of donated Health 
Products and Technologies in supporting public health initiatives, 
especially in resource-limited settings, the PPB aims to ensure that 
all donations meet the necessary safety, quality, and efficacy 
standards. 
Globally, the practice of donating Health Products and Technologies 
plays a critical role in improving access to essential medicines and 
Technologies in supporting healthcare systems during emergencies 
or routine operations. However, the donation of substandard or 
inappropriate products can burden healthcare systems, introduce 
safety risks, and lead to inefficiencies. To mitigate these challenges, 
the World Health Organization (WHO) and other health organizations 
advocate for strict guidelines to govern donations, ensuring they are 
beneficial, ethical, and aligned with recipient needs. 
This Donation Guideline aligns with the WHO’s Guidelines for Drug 
Donations and emphasizes the following key principles: 
1. Quality Assurance: All donated products must meet national 
and international quality standards to protect patient safety. 
2. Relevance and Suitability: Donations should meet the 
specific needs of the population and be aligned with the 
national essential medicines list. 
3. Transparent Process: The guideline outlines a transparent, 
efficient process for submission, review, and approval of 
donations, promoting equity and accountability. 
4. Sustainability: Donations should be integrated into the 
healthcare 
system 
without 
creating 
dependency 
or 
 
 
 
2
operational strain. 
The guideline serves as a reference document for applicants—such 
as pharmaceutical companies, non-governmental organizations 
(NGOs), and international agencies—seeking to donate Health 
Products and Technologies within the country. It provides clear steps 
for submission, documentation, quality checks, and final approval. 
Additionally, it ensures that all donations are received ethically, 
responsibly, and with a focus on maximizing public health benefit. 
Expedited customs clearance is required for all donated medicines. 
Customs and PPB officials managing medicine donations have the 
responsibility to facilitate entry of approved donations. The criteria 
for acceptance of donations is as advised by this guideline.  
1.2 
Legal Framework/Responsibility for Implementation 
The Pharmacy and Poisons Board (PPB) is the Drug Regulatory 
Authority established under the Pharmacy and Poisons Act, Chapter 
244 of the Laws of Kenya. The Board is responsible for granting or 
withdrawing marketing authorization for Health Products and 
Technologies, subject to appropriate conditions, and reviewing such 
conditions as necessary. 
The Board in its mandate under section 3(b), ensures that all 
medicinal products and technologies manufactured in, imported into 
or exported from the country conform to prescribed standards of 
quality, safety and efficacy. Therefore, this guideline serves to offer 
quality assurance principles on HPTs donated by well-wishers into 
Kenyan territory. 
Furthermore, Rule 16(12) of the Pharmacy and Poisons Rules 2022, 
has a provision for the donation of unregistered health products and 
technologies.  
In specific circumstances, there are compelling reasons to import 
donations of Health Products and Technologies (HPTs). Therefore, 
this guide provides detailed instructions for applying for such 
 
 
 
3
donations in Kenya. 
1.3 
Scope 
The Donation Guidelines apply to all entities seeking to donate 
Health Products and Technologies and technologies to healthcare 
facilities and programs. This includes, but is not limited to, the 
following: 
Pharmaceutical 
companies, 
non-governmental 
organizations (NGOs), International aid organizations, Private and 
public sector entities and Individual donors. 
The types of Health Products and Technologies covered include and 
are not limited to, prescription only medicines (POM) and over-the-
counter medicines (OTC), medical devices and diagnostic equipment, 
Vaccines and biologicals, Consumables and medical supplies, and 
indeed any other health-related products intended for use in 
healthcare settings. 
This guideline is intended to ensure that all medical product 
donations are of good quality, safe, efficacious/effective, and aligned 
with the healthcare needs and standards of the receiving facilities 
and communities. It provides a transparent and standardized 
process, helping both donors and recipients maximize the benefit of 
medical product donations to support public health outcomes. 
Further this guideline does not cover donations of returned health 
products and technologies (unused medicines returned to a 
pharmacy for safe disposal and/or free samples given to health 
professionals) owing to quality and safety concerns.

1.4 
Objectives 
The objectives of these guidelines are:

c) To ensure that the Health Products and Technologies donated 
are safe, of good quality and effective/efficacious. 
d) To make sure that donated Health Products and Technologies 
are in compliance with the needs of the country. These

for submitting eligible Health Products and Technologies for

PRINCIPLES AND GENERAL REQUIREMENTS  
2.1 
General principles for a good donation; 
2.1.1 
All donations for HPTs into the Country must be 
authorized by the Board. 
2.1.2 
HPTs being donated into the country need not to be 
registered locally but their quality attributes in respect 
to 
quality, 
safety, 
efficacy/effectiveness 
and 
performance must be demonstratable, verifiable and 
traceable. It shall be the responsibility of the recipient 
health establishment in Kenya to ensure that this is 
provided when called upon by the Board. 
2.1.3 
The Board shall leverage on reliance and recognition 
mechanisms that have already been established in the 
management of donations. 
2.1.4 
The donated HPTs must comply with quality standards 
of the donor country. 
2.1.5 
The donation of HPTs shall benefit the recipient of the 
donation and the clients/patients shall be served to the 
maximum extent possible. 
2.1.6 
For HPT(s) registered in Kenya, the applicant shall 
attach a Letter of No Objection from the Marketing 
Authorization Holder or Local Technical Representative 
allowing for the importation of the donation(s). 
2.1.7 
A donation shall be given with full respect of the needs 
of the recipient organization and without violation of 
provisions of the Pharmacy and Poisons Act on quality, 
safety, efficacy and performance of HPT’s. For example, 
if the quality, safety, efficacy or performance of an item 
is unacceptable in the country from which it originates, 
it shall be deemed unacceptable as a donation into 
Kenya.

and relevance to the disease pattern in the country 
(This may be waived in acute emergencies);  
2.1.9 
It is the responsibility of the recipient institution or

donations and those which may arrive unannounced 
and unwanted. 
2.1.10 Donations may include products which have market 
authorization in Kenya or those without market 
authorization in Kenya; 
2.1.11 Donations must not be sent into Kenya unannounced. 
At all times, there shall be sufficient communication 
that is understood by all parties involved in the 
donation process. Where needed, the Ministry of

Pharmacy and Poisons Board may be consulted to offer 
any technical guidance; 
2.1.12 The donation application process is considered a pre-
shipment activity; HPT(s) should not be imported 
before approval by the Board.

2.1.14 In certain circumstances and in accordance with

5.0 
PHARMACOVIGILANCE AND POST-MARKET SURVEILLANCE 
REPORTING 
5.1 
Existing 
pharmacovigilance 
and 
post-marketing 
surveillance 
mechanisms at the National Pharmacovigilance Centre shall apply to 
the collection and dissemination of data related to quality, safety, and 
efficacy information.  
5.2 
All adverse events shall be documented and reported as detailed in 
the Guidelines on Safety and Vigilance of Health Products and 
Technologies, January 2024 (HPT/PDS/VMS/GUD/030). 
5.3 
Any such reports should include concomitant medication(s) used. 
5.4 
In addition, information on patients treated with any of the medicines 
during pregnancy shall be reported with follow-up on the outcome of 
the pregnancy. 
5.5 
For donations where a Local Technical Representative (LTR) or 
Marketing Authorization Holder (MAH) is present, the Qualified 
Person Responsible for Pharmacovigilance (QPPV) shall ensure safety 
monitoring and maintain a robust pharmacovigilance system. 
5.6 
For donations channeled through the Ministry of Health or other 
governmental organizations, a designated pharmacovigilance focal 
person shall be appointed to oversee all pharmacovigilance activities 
related to the donated products and technologies in the facilities 
where the HPTs shall be used. 
5.7 
For any other donations received through other channels other than 
those stated above (Churches, Medical camps etc), a focal/contact 
person shall be appointed to oversee all the pharmacovigilance 
activities 
5.8 
The consignee of donated products shall have in place a system to 
monitor and respond to any quality, safety and efficacy concerns of 
the donated HPTs.  
5.9 
The recipient (consignee in Kenya) of donations and unregistered 
medicinal substances shall be responsible for implementing 
regulatory actions, such as quarantine, recalls or withdrawals of the 
 
 
 
19
donated HPTs as required by Guidelines for Recall and Withdrawal 
of Health Products and Technologies (HPT/PDS/VMS/GUD/030) 
6.0 
DISPOSAL OR WITHDRAWAL OF DONATED HPT(S) 
6.1 
Management of expired products and where applicable reverse 
logistics of expired products must be agreed in advance. In principle 
the recipient should accept responsibility for the disposal or 
withdrawal of donated medicines when necessary.  
6.2 
Recipients should ensure that a system is in place for collecting and 
processing complaints about donated products. 
6.3 
The Sustainable Waste Management Act CAP 387C and Pharmacy 
and Poisons Rules (Waste Management) 2022 should be followed  
6.4 
Where national laws or guidance are insufficient, WHO’s Guidelines 
for safe disposal of unwanted pharmaceuticals in and after 
emergencies should be followed. 
6.5 
The PPB guidelines for recall and withdrawal of Health Products and 
Technologies and health technologies (HPT/PDS/VMS/GUD/030) 
should be followed in the event a poor-quality product needs to be 
recalled.  
7.0 
MIXED DONATION APPLICATIONS. 
7.1 
Donation consignments consisting of a combination of medicines 
and medical devices and/or other HPT(s) as outlined in this 
guideline shall be properly accounted for, shall be safe for use, and 
shall be aligned with public health objectives. The respective 
documentation detailed in the class specific requirements for each 
product category as highlighted under section 3 of this guideline 
should be adhered to. 
7.2 
Separate packaging is recommended for medicines and medical 
devices within a single consignment including the use of distinct 
outer boxes or crates labeled accordingly (e.g., "Medicines" and 
"Medical Devices") to facilitate easy identification and minimize the 
risk of cross-contamination or damage during transit.  
 
 
 
20
7.3 
As far as practicable, each packaging type should include its 
own Packing List to maintain clear documentation for both the 
customs and PPB to ensure that all items can be quickly and 
accurately identified and processed by the recipient organization.
